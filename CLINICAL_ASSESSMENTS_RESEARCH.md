## 1️⃣ SYMPTOM ASSESSMENT TOOLS

### **ESAS (Edmonton Symptom Assessment System)**
**Most widely used symptom assessment in palliative care**

**What it measures:**
- Pain (0-10)
- Tiredness (0-10)
- Drowsiness (0-10)
- Nausea (0-10)
- Lack of appetite (0-10)
- Depression (0-10)
- Anxiety (0-10)
- Shortness of breath (0-10)
- Wellbeing (0-10)
- Optional 10th symptom

**Scoring:**
- 0 = No symptom
- 10 = Worst possible symptom
- Each symptom rated independently
- Higher scores indicate worse symptoms

**Frequency:** Daily to weekly depending on patient status

**Validation:** 
- Validated in multiple countries ([Source: NIH](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5337174/))
- High reliability and validity
- Used in 1000+ palliative care centers worldwide
- Suitable for self-report or proxy completion

**Clinical Use:**
- Track symptom trends over time
- Identify deterioration early
- Guide treatment adjustments
- Monitor treatment effectiveness

## 2️⃣ COGNITIVE & MEMORY ASSESSMENT

### **MMSE (Mini-Mental State Examination)**
**Gold standard cognitive screening**

**What it measures:**
- Orientation to time (5 points)
- Orientation to place (5 points)
- Registration (3 points)
- Attention and calculation (5 points)
- Recall (3 points)
- Language (8 points)
- Visual construction (1 point)

**Scoring:**
- Total: 30 points
- 24-30: Normal cognition
- 18-23: Mild cognitive impairment
- 0-17: Severe cognitive impairment

**Time to complete:** 5-10 minutes

**Validation:**
- Validated since 1975
- Most widely used cognitive test
- Prognostic value in palliative care ([Source: Springer](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs00520-010-1028-1))

**Limitations:**
- Less sensitive for mild impairment
- Education and age affect scores

---

### **MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment)**
**More sensitive than MMSE for mild impairment**

**What it measures:**
- Visuospatial/executive (5 points)
- Naming (3 points)
- Memory (0 points for delayed recall)
- Attention (6 points)
- Language (3 points)
- Abstraction (2 points)
- Delayed recall (5 points)
- Orientation (6 points)

**Scoring:**
- Total: 30 points
- ≥26: Normal
- 18-25: Mild cognitive impairment
- 10-17: Moderate impairment
- <10: Severe impairment
- Add 1 point if ≤12 years education

**Time to complete:** 10-15 minutes

**Validation:**
- Detects 90% of MCI vs 18% for MMSE ([Source: ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7919702))
- Better prognostic value in oncology patients
- Higher sensitivity maintained in palliative care

**Advantages over MMSE:**
- Better executive function assessment
- More sensitive to mild changes
- Better predictor of survival

---

### **Clock Drawing Test**
**Quick visuospatial and executive function test**

**What it measures:**
- Ability to draw clock face
- Place numbers correctly
- Set hands to specific time

**Scoring:**
- Various scoring systems (0-10 or 0-5)
- Assesses planning, visual memory, executive function

**Time to complete:** 2-3 minutes

**Use:** Quick screening, can be combined with other tests

## 3️⃣ FUNCTIONAL STATUS ASSESSMENT

### **PPS (Palliative Performance Scale)**
**Gold standard for palliative care functional assessment**

**What it measures (5 domains):**
1. **Ambulation** (ability to walk)
2. **Activity & evidence of disease**
3. **Self-care** (independence)
4. **Oral intake**
5. **Level of consciousness**

**Scoring:**
- 100%: Full ambulation, normal activity, no disease evidence
- 90%: Normal activity with effort, some disease evidence
- 80%: Normal activity with effort, extensive disease
- 70%: Reduced ambulation, unable to work, significant disease
- 60%: Reduced ambulation, unable to hobbies, extensive disease
- 50%: Mainly sitting/lying, unable to work, extensive disease
- 40%: Mainly in bed, limited self-care
- 30%: Totally bed bound, minimal self-care
- 20%: Totally bed bound, minimal intake
- 10%: Death imminent, minimal intake
- 0%: Death

**Frequency:** Weekly or at change in status

**Validation:**
- Modified from Karnofsky Scale specifically for palliative care ([Source: ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/14348689))
- Strong prognostic value
- Correlates with survival time
- Inter-rater reliability >0.9

**Clinical Use:**
- Prognosis estimation
- Care planning decisions
- Track functional decline
- Hospice eligibility determination

---

### **Barthel Index (ADL Assessment)**
**Activities of Daily Living independence**

**What it measures (10 activities):**
1. Feeding (0, 5, 10)
2. Bathing (0, 5)
3. Grooming (0, 5)
4. Dressing (0, 5, 10)
5. Bowel control (0, 5, 10)
6. Bladder control (0, 5, 10)
7. Toilet use (0, 5, 10)
8. Transfers (bed/chair) (0, 5, 10, 15)
9. Mobility (0, 5, 10, 15)
10. Stairs (0, 5, 10)

**Scoring:**
- Total: 0-100
- 100: Fully independent
- 80-99: Minimal dependence
- 60-79: Partial dependence
- 40-59: Moderate dependence
- 20-39: Severe dependence
- 0-19: Total dependence

**Time to complete:** 5-10 minutes

**Validation:**
- Used since 1965 ([Source: Physiopedia](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Barthel_Index))
- High reliability
- Predicts discharge outcomes
- Suitable for dementia patients

**Clinical Use:**
- Determine care needs
- Track functional improvement/decline
- Guide discharge planning
- Caregiver workload assessment

---

### **IADL (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living)**
**Complex daily living skills**

**What it measures (8 activities):**
1. Using telephone
2. Shopping
3. Food preparation
4. Housekeeping
5. Laundry
6. Transportation
7. Medication management
8. Financial management

**Scoring:**
- 0-8 scale
- Higher score = more independent
- Each activity scored independently

**Use:** For patients with higher function, community dwelling

## 4️⃣ PSYCHOLOGICAL & EMOTIONAL ASSESSMENT

### **PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire - Depression)**
**Validated depression screening**

**What it measures (9 symptoms, past 2 weeks):**
1. Little interest or pleasure
2. Feeling down, depressed, hopeless
3. Trouble sleeping or sleeping too much
4. Feeling tired or having little energy
5. Poor appetite or overeating
6. Feeling bad about yourself
7. Trouble concentrating
8. Moving/speaking slowly or being fidgety
9. Thoughts of self-harm

**Scoring:**
- 0-27 scale (each item 0-3)
- 0-4: Minimal depression
- 5-9: Mild depression
- 10-14: Moderate depression
- 15-19: Moderately severe depression
- 20-27: Severe depression

**Time to complete:** 2-3 minutes

**Validation:**
- Widely validated across populations
- High sensitivity and specificity
- Used in primary care and palliative care
- Can be repeated weekly

**Clinical Use:**
- Screen for depression
- Monitor treatment response
- Identify need for psychiatric referral

---

### **GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder Scale)**
**Validated anxiety screening**

**What it measures (7 symptoms, past 2 weeks):**
1. Feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge
2. Not being able to stop worrying
3. Worrying too much about different things
4. Trouble relaxing
5. Being so restless it's hard to sit still
6. Becoming easily annoyed or irritable
7. Feeling afraid something awful might happen

**Scoring:**
- 0-21 scale (each item 0-3)
- 0-4: Minimal anxiety
- 5-9: Mild anxiety
- 10-14: Moderate anxiety
- 15-21: Severe anxiety

**Time to complete:** 2-3 minutes

**Validation:**
- High reliability and validity
- Correlates with other anxiety measures
- Can be used with PHQ-9 for combined assessment

**Clinical Use:**
- Screen for anxiety disorders
- Monitor anxiety levels
- Guide treatment decisions

---

### **HADS (Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale)**
**Designed specifically for medical patients**

**What it measures:**
- 7 anxiety questions (HADS-A)
- 7 depression questions (HADS-D)
- Excludes somatic symptoms (fatigue, sleep issues)

**Scoring:**
- Each subscale: 0-21
- 0-7: Normal
- 8-10: Borderline abnormal (mild)
- 11-21: Abnormal (moderate-severe)

**Time to complete:** 5 minutes

**Validation:**
- Validated in cancer and palliative care ([Source: PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20207007/))
- Sensitivity 82%, Specificity 77% for depression
- Avoids confounding from physical symptoms

**Advantages:**
- Designed for physically ill patients
- Doesn't overlap with disease symptoms
- Quick and easy

## 5️⃣ PAIN ASSESSMENT TOOLS

### **NRS (Numerical Rating Scale)**
**Simplest pain assessment**

**What it measures:**
- Pain intensity on 0-10 scale
- 0 = No pain
- 1-3 = Mild pain
- 4-6 = Moderate pain
- 7-9 = Severe pain
- 10 = Worst possible pain

**Frequency:** Multiple times daily in acute settings

**Validation:**
- Most widely used pain scale
- Easy to administer
- Can be verbal or visual

---

### **VAS (Visual Analog Scale)**
**Continuous pain measurement**

**What it measures:**
- 100mm line from "no pain" to "worst pain"
- Patient marks current pain level
- Measured in millimeters (0-100)

**Use:** Research settings, detailed pain tracking

---

### **McGill Pain Questionnaire**
**Comprehensive pain assessment**

**What it measures:**
- Sensory qualities (throbbing, shooting, stabbing)
- Affective qualities (tiring, sickening, fearful)
- Evaluative qualities (annoying, miserable, intense)
- Pain intensity (Present Pain Intensity)

**Scoring:** Complex multidimensional scoring

**Time to complete:** 10-15 minutes

**Use:** Comprehensive pain assessment, research

---

### **Brief Pain Inventory (BPI)**
**Pain and its impact on function**

**What it measures:**
- Pain severity (4 items): worst, least, average, current
- Pain interference (7 items): activity, mood, walking, work, relations, sleep, enjoyment

**Scoring:**
- Severity: 0-10 average of 4 items
- Interference: 0-10 average of 7 items

**Time to complete:** 5 minutes

**Validation:**
- Validated in cancer patients
- Assesses both pain and impact
- Widely used in oncology

---

## 6️⃣ QUALITY OF LIFE ASSESSMENT

### **EORTC QLQ-C30**
**European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer**

**What it measures:**
- Functional scales (5): Physical, role, emotional, cognitive, social
- Symptom scales (9): Fatigue, pain, nausea/vomiting, dyspnea, insomnia, appetite, constipation, diarrhea, financial difficulties
- Global health/QOL scale (2)

**Scoring:** 0-100 scale for each domain

**Time to complete:** 10-15 minutes

**Validation:**
- Gold standard in oncology QOL
- Validated in 80+ countries
- Disease-specific modules available

---

### **FACT-G (Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy)**
**Comprehensive QOL for cancer patients**

**What it measures (4 domains):**
- Physical wellbeing (7 items)
- Social/family wellbeing (7 items)
- Emotional wellbeing (6 items)
- Functional wellbeing (7 items)

**Scoring:**
- Total 0-108
- Higher scores = better QOL

**Time to complete:** 5-10 minutes

**Disease-specific versions:** FACT-L (lung), FACT-B (breast), etc.

---

## 7️⃣ MOTOR SKILLS & PHYSICAL ASSESSMENT

### **Grip Strength Test**
**Objective physical strength measure**

**What it measures:**
- Hand grip strength in kg using dynamometer
- Best of 3 attempts for each hand

**Scoring:**
- Compare to age/gender norms
- Declining grip strength predicts mortality
- <16kg (women) or <26kg (men) indicates weakness

**Validation:**
- Strong predictor of frailty
- Correlates with overall strength
- Easy objective measure

---

### **Timed Up and Go (TUG)**
**Mobility and fall risk assessment**

**What it measures:**
- Time to stand from chair, walk 3 meters, turn, return, sit
- Uses assistive devices if normally used

**Scoring:**
- <10 seconds: Normal
- 10-20 seconds: Good mobility
- 20-30 seconds: Mobility problems
- >30 seconds: High fall risk

**Time to complete:** 1-2 minutes

**Validation:**
- Predicts falls risk
- Assesses functional mobility
- Easy to administer

---

### **6-Minute Walk Test**
**Cardiopulmonary functional capacity**

**What it measures:**
- Distance walked in 6 minutes
- Vital signs before/after
- Dyspnea and fatigue rating

**Scoring:**
- Compare to predicted normal
- <350m indicates poor prognosis

**Use:** Patients with cardiac/respiratory disease

---

## 8️⃣ CAREGIVER BURDEN ASSESSMENT

### **Zarit Burden Interview**
**Gold standard caregiver burden assessment**

**What it measures (22 items):**
- Impact of caregiving on caregiver's life
- Physical health
- Mental health
- Financial status
- Social life
- Relationship with patient

**Scoring:**
- 0-88 scale
- 0-20: Little or no burden
- 21-40: Mild to moderate burden
- 41-60: Moderate to severe burden
- 61-88: Severe burden

**Time to complete:** 10 minutes

**Validation:**
- Most widely used caregiver measure
- Predicts caregiver breakdown
- Guides support interventions

## 🎮 GAME-BASED ASSESSMENTS

### **Digit Span Test** (Memory)
- Repeat sequences of numbers forward/backward
- Tests working memory
- 3-7 minutes

### **Trail Making Test** (Executive Function)
- Connect numbered circles (Part A)
- Connect alternating numbers/letters (Part B)
- Tests visual attention, task switching
- 5-10 minutes

### **Word Fluency Test** (Language/Executive)
- Name as many words starting with a letter in 1 minute
- Category fluency (animals, fruits)
- Tests language, executive function
- 3-5 minutes

### **Memory Card Matching**
- Digital version of concentration game
- Tests visual memory
- Engaging for patients

## 📅 RECOMMENDED ASSESSMENT SCHEDULE

### **At Admission:**
- PPS (Functional Status)
- ESAS (Symptoms)
- NRS or BPI (Pain)
- PHQ-9 & GAD-7 (Mental Health)
- MMSE or MoCA (Cognitive)
- Barthel Index (ADL)

### **Weekly:**
- PPS
- ESAS
- NRS

### **Bi-weekly:**
- PHQ-9
- GAD-7

### **Monthly:**
- Barthel Index
- Cognitive tests (if relevant)
- QOL measures

### **At Change in Status:**
- All relevant assessments
- Trigger AI alerts if scores worsen

## 🤖 AI INTEGRATION FEATURES

### **Automatic Alerts:**
- ESAS pain >7 → Pain management alert
- PPS decline >10% → Functional decline alert
- PHQ-9 >15 → Psychiatric referral alert
- MMSE decline >3 points → Cognitive decline alert

### **Trend Analysis:**
- Graph all scores over time
- Detect deteriorating patterns
- Predict decline before crisis

### **Personalized Recommendations:**
- Suggest interventions based on scores
- Adapt care plans automatically
- Resource recommendations per domain

### **Caregiver Support:**
- Zarit Burden >40 → Respite care recommendation
- Workload balancing suggestions
- Support group referrals
